WritHer vs Wispr Flow

Looking for a free, offline alternative to Wispr Flow on Windows? Compare the trade-offs, not just the feature lists.

Updated 24 October 2026

Choose WritHer when you want local dictation on Windows and source code you can adapt. Consider Wispr Flow when its supported platforms, managed experience or team features fit your needs.

Different approaches to voice typing

WritHer runs speech recognition on your PC with faster-whisper. You choose the speech model and can connect a local language-model server for notes, reminders and appointments. The software is MIT-licensed and free to modify.

Wispr Flow's official pricing page lists dictation on Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android, with free and paid plans. Its privacy documentation describes sending audio to its servers for transcription and settings that control storage and model training.

What “offline” means for WritHer

Download the speech model during setup. After that, dictation can run without an internet connection. Assistant mode also works offline if its server and model are on your machine. A remote provider URL sends assistant requests to that endpoint.

Local processing gives you control, but it also makes speed and model capacity dependent on your PC. WritHer does not promise the same performance on every machine. Start with dictation, choose a suitable model and add the assistant separately.

A fair look at pricing and privacy

Wispr Flow currently lists a free plan, so it would be misleading to describe it as paid-only. Plans and limits can change; consult Wispr Flow's current pricing instead of relying on an old price or quota.

Cloud processing is not the same as retaining audio for training. Wispr Flow documents separate controls for model improvement, cloud history and local history. Read its privacy controls and data flow when making that decision. WritHer's approach is to let you run the speech model and assistant locally.

Who WritHer is for

WritHer is a good fit if you work on Windows, value local speech recognition and enjoy a tool you can personalize. It adds custom vocabulary, optional symbol dictation and a local assistant for everyday notes and schedules. It is also a project you can help shape.

If you need a supported mobile app or managed organizational features, evaluate a product that already provides them. WritHer currently focuses on Windows; other-platform contributions are welcome, but are not a shipped capability.

Try your own workflow

Use a short email, a paragraph of notes and a few specialist terms to compare the tools on work you actually do. Check accuracy, correction effort, setup time, hardware requirements and where your data is processed.

Follow the WritHer Windows setup guide, browse the FAQ, or join the community. You can contribute an improvement even if you decide a different tool fits your daily work.
